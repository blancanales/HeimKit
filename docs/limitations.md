# Safety and limitations

This page collects HeimKit's gameplay, client and diagnostic limitations. Feature guides describe controls and supported behavior; specifications define implementation contracts.

## World safety

Use HeimKit at your own risk. Mods can corrupt worlds or cause data loss; we are not responsible for resulting damage. Back up the world before installing, updating or enabling HeimKit. Stop the server before replacing its DLL, editing configuration or copying save files. Follow [world backups](world-backups.md), including SharedMap's separate data directory.

## Server observations and logs

HeimKit works from server-observed state. Client ownership, unloaded objects and replication delays affect when changes are visible. Logs distinguish requests, accepted state and observed results; they do not confirm a player's screen or delivery to every client. Ready means native services are available. Compilation, compatibility checks and hook registration do not establish gameplay correctness.

Some diagnostic observers have incomplete incident repeat-count/recovery accounting. Debug output is sampled and capped. Missing detail lines do not establish missing processing. A replication sender may be forwarding another player's action, so sender identity does not prove who clicked a control. Other mods that patch the same systems can conflict. The manual game-version override changes the accepted version, not the API audit. See [supported environment](server-setup.md#supported-environment).

## SharedMap

### Discovery

Exploration uses periodic, replicated player positions. Sampling can miss travel between observations; teleports reveal endpoint circles only. Dead, unavailable and interior-height positions do not contribute. Camera visibility is not evaluated.

Resource, dungeon, runestone and spawner discovery waits for native records. An installed prefab does not mean the location or deposit exists in a particular world. Unplaced dungeon centers do not establish exterior entrances. Retrospective discovery uses accumulated community exploration, including imported table terrain; enabling it can reveal previously visited locations without a revisit.

Silver mining discovery depends on authenticated impacts or correlated digging observations. Missing baselines, delayed data or simultaneous nearby tools can leave a vein hidden until another qualifying impact. Missing/unloaded resource records do not prove depletion; unobserved destruction can leave historical markers. Copper/Silver percentages measure remaining mining sections, not ore yield, exposed volume or burial.

A dungeon percentage measures only its listed permanent pickup sites, not creature kills, chest contents or overall dungeon completion. Troll Cave markers do not report whether their Troll was defeated. Picking a site releases its item; it does not prove the item reached an inventory. Interior visits use sampled, living player positions and a unique saved location in the native interior zone; ambiguous matches do not unlock percentages. Matched already-picked items can also establish a prior visit; they do not identify who entered or when. Empty pickup records require a settling period after entry because replication can arrive late. Missing layouts, missing previously established records and unsupported objectives remain unknown. Respawning mushrooms/Jelly, random chest contents and destroyed pickups are excluded. Percentages already copied to personal maps by an earlier build cannot be withdrawn by the server.

Runestones use validated placed-proxy hierarchy offsets. Native StaticPhysics may settle their height locally; their horizontal landmark point remains fixed. Their static lore objects have no individual destruction record. Spawner markers require visible, player-destructible physical objects with actual persistent source records; an installed or historically registered prefab does not prove world presence. Only accepted source-ZDO destruction is retirement evidence. Destruction missed while processing is paused, before compatible metadata is available, or while the plugin is absent can leave a historical marker. Killing spawned creatures and scene unloading never retire a source. Spawner vertical settling retains the accepted source height; simultaneously stacked sources sharing one horizontal identity are omitted as ambiguous. Exact source reassociation rejects ambiguity; moved/replaced sources without a unique accepted identity retain the last state.

Interior runestones/spawners are omitted: publication requires verified saved-layout membership and an established exterior gateway association, which this marker path does not provide. High-altitude coordinates are never projected onto the outdoor map. Outdoor placement of an otherwise eligible physical source can qualify; this does not imply complete interior support. Unsupported animated/conditional hierarchies and excessive replicated scales are omitted.

### Client map updates

Players receive updates through Read Map after table reconciliation and replication. An early read may use older data. The server cannot force an open map to refresh or edit a personal client map.

Vanilla matches an incoming marker to any saved pin within one horizontal metre, without updating its label or icon. Personal pins can block insertion; distinct entrances closer than that can appear as one client pin. SharedMap additionally suppresses dungeon publications within ten horizontal metres of a stable actual-point anchor. Recognized dungeon-labeled hammer copies in that area are cleaned from shared table data; genuine exceptionally close entrances can therefore share one publication while retaining separate histories. Local stale-pin deletion followed by Read Map may be necessary. Portal/resource rename offsets and dungeon percentage/shared-checkmark offsets encourage replacement, but missed updates, reused slots, personal pins and regrouping can retain stale labels or checkmarks. Checkmark changes require allowed Write Map uploads; a stale or unchanged upload is not an explicit reversal. Personal pins belonging to the reader remain under that player's control. Publication offsets add at most about 2.83 metres; portal/resource rename offsets can add their own displacement.

### Player uploads

Write Map reads the table before uploading. Deleting a pin locally may therefore re-import it; later uploads from another player can restore it. Use [selected-pin removal](features/shared-map.md#removing-an-unwanted-shared-pin) for persistent table exclusions.

Checked pins are client-local until Write Map. With a client-owned table, the server may see its owner forwarding another player's upload. Native owner/author/display-name fields, proximity and table ownership cannot authenticate who clicked X. Automatic admin checked-pin deletion is unavailable; use server-side selected-pin administration. Shared X marks remain governed by SharePinCheckmarks. Table removals reach other players through shared data; already copied personal pins and the uploader's adopted X remain client-owned and may need local removal. Unrecognized ownership edits are preserved as ordinary pins rather than authorizing a global deletion.

AllowPlayerMapWrites=false rejects incoming server map fields. Vanilla still displays Write Map, its effects and its success message. A client owner can complete a local write/read before server correction; rejection cannot undo that read.

### Saved data

Community terrain, discoveries and marker state are separate from the native world save. A crash can lose changes since the last completed save. Restoring an older world with newer community data can reveal later progress. Corrupt or incompatible stores disable SharedMap instead of silently resetting data. Back up and restore matching files together.

## AFK detection, sleep and raids

Idle is an inference, not proof of human absence. Camera-only movement and unsent menu, map or inventory actions can be invisible. Unknown or unreliable observations retain native eligibility and restart inference grace. Sleep still depends on native bed/time checks; a permitted vote does not prove that a skip completed. Raid exclusion counts describe candidate filtering, not a canceled raid or a raid that was certain to occur. Ordinary enemies and existing raids remain active.

## WeatherGuard and InfiniteTorches

WeatherGuard coverage depends on loaded server context and ownership. Roof samples and coverage reports describe their stated regions and ages, not the whole world. Server weather is not a measurement of each client's local rain or Wet status; prevention waits for native damage timing.

Torch refilling can wait for loaded records, ownership and replication. Late delivery can overlap timeout retries. Native water, smoke and obstruction checks still affect whether a fueled light burns. Simultaneous manual refilling can consume its normal material, and an observed fuel increase cannot uniquely identify which operation caused it.

## TameTravel

Validated portal traversal combines fresh authenticated following, source trigger geometry, unchanged portal links and matching arrival. Unmodified clients publish positions rather than a portal-use event. An owner-local administrative teleport that reproduces the same observations can be indistinguishable. The public map animates markers toward position updates; its fast motion is not a physical travel path. Map visibility is not required for TameTravel.

Accepted owner updates are retained between controller passes, but movement absent from replication cannot be reconstructed. A fresh inside-trigger point can establish source presence without an observed outside boundary. Stale or overflowing history and a jump without source evidence withhold travel. Dense rosters can exceed the bounded processing window. A six-second retained source witness accommodates some asynchronous native portal-link updates; unresolved or changing links and ambiguous usable triggers still withhold transfer. A cancelled attempt consumes optional single-trip waiting consent. Active follow permission survives an attempt while the same valid player session and native following remain; neither follow permission nor interrupted transfers are reconstructed after reconnect or restart.

Arrival timing, continued presence, reference position and native ping echoes constrain readiness but do not expose completion of the client's loading screen. Narrow trigger crossings, packet delays, dense player rosters, missing terrain/scene objects, duplicate names or insufficient animal-sized clearance can withhold transfer. A follow request is not a confirmed result. Chat communication restrictions, recipient routing and owner-local wolf commands can hide input from the server. Use the vanilla Come Here/No No No/Wave emotes to provide replicated nearby follow/stay/wait input. Unknown custom slash commands never reach the server. An explicit follow association is required; standing livestock never gains permission from proximity alone. Remaining followers can stay at the source when a journey times out or the player leaves the destination area. A pending stop revokes travel permission before native stay confirmation, which can still fail if ownership, the player instance or native command handling is unavailable. An unresolved earlier follow command also withholds stay confirmation rather than treating its initial empty state as success.

Ownership barriers preserve the same animal record and fence stale updates during transfer; the forced native source-record return reconciles cached persistent data, while private state absent from that record remains unavailable. Interactions with reserved animals pause briefly during the handoff. Recorded relocation, ordered message reception and observed destination-owner simulation are different outcomes. Interrupted handoffs can leave the original animal at its destination without confirmed resumed simulation; they never authorize a replacement copy. Native world saving owns final location and animal data, so crashes/restored saves can roll back recent travel. No consent or interrupted journey is resumed after restart.

Native following uses a fixed three-dimensional centre distance. TameTravel can replace that movement intent only while it owns an explicitly commanded follower and has ready terrain/collision context. Capacity refusal, a failed source-state drain, riding, player separation beyond thirty-two metres or lost context releases control to native behavior. A received follow confirmation alone does not establish active spacing; a separate HUD/log result identifies activation. Adjusted spacing does not prevent combat approaches, knockback, all collision pushes, or resolve animal pathfinding through narrow doors and stairs. Brief ownership/context transitions pause simulation and interactions; repeated commands during those transitions may be withheld. Portal waiting avoids needing an animal to pathfind into a building. Native stay/patrol can wander. Native physics and pathfinding now run on the server for retained followers; controller and follow-movement timings do not measure all of that cost. The follow-movement timer includes synchronous native MoveTo work, but excludes other AI, physics and work scheduled later. Native private client AI caches are not copied by record draining.

Rejected command selection appears in TameTravel Debug evidence; emote start/stop state can still be coalesced by client replication. `/nonono` addresses only the sender's saved followers or current associations within thirty-two metres and does not grant portal permission after reconnect.

TameTravel samples arcs around the traveller out to twelve metres before wider bounded grids. Padding and its push use only nearby colliders and horizontal penetration; opposing walls in a narrow gap cancel the push, and spots crowded by more than thirty-two colliders are rejected. Low structural paving may support a landing; furniture, tall construction, overhead pieces and unsupported footprints do not. Narrow gaps between samples, the deadline, missing collision context or unavailable native navigation can still withhold placement despite visible space. The access check uses a player-sized native route with current physical wall checks and a bounded detour allowance. It permits building doorways, but does not prove the animal itself can fit through those doorways or stairs. Landing checks also cover its actual body, footprint and a bounded walkout; they cannot prevent later obstruction by players, creatures or construction. A server-record or ownership acknowledgement does not prove that a particular client has rendered the animal.


## New world features

DoorKeeper, ForestRenewal and SeaSalvage work from server-observed state and require ready native world context. Loading, dense sector scopes, missing prefab definitions, ownership changes and replication delays can postpone work. Rotating player scopes bound processing; many players or large builds can increase delays. Position and movement-segment exclusion checks cannot predict an unreported player movement or guarantee what a client currently sees.

DoorKeeper retains native surrounding objects before taking door ownership so native support, weather and damage handlers have their context. Preparation can take multiple native scene-creation frames. Manual interactions during ownership transitions still depend on native delivery. Close timing begins from observed open state and resets after controller recreation; it is not an exact measurement of when a player clicked. Door clearance automatically expands a conservative sphere around the native collision bounds for wide/tall gates; this can delay closure until players move farther away.

ForestRenewal discovers standing stumps and observes native stump destruction while occupied. Old stumps start their timer when first observed. A pending standing stump that disappears without an observed destruction event waits; missed removals are not reconstructed from absence. Structure buffers use conservative saved collision bounds and are rechecked at sapling placement, including new buildings during the delay. Unsupported/missing geometry defers work; this is not continuous enforcement around later construction or existing mature trees. Paint exclusions cover detectable dirt/paved/cultivated ground, not every player-used path. The separate world-specific renewal ledger must be backed up with the world. Consuming a renewal before placing its sapling prevents retries from duplicating it; a crash before the subsequent native world save can forfeit that renewal. Corrupt ledgers stop renewal. Restoring mismatched world/ledger snapshots can suppress later renewals. Persisted decisions are bounded; reaching the ledger capacity skips new stump observations/removals. Failed native plant growth is not refunded or bypassed.

ForestRenewal rechecks placement exclusions after durable acknowledgement. Confirmed new exclusions cancel renewal; missing context before any world mutation returns the original decision to pending after sixty seconds without rerolling. Orderly shutdown returns unperformed placements to pending and waits for outstanding ledger I/O; normal gameplay does not wait on disk flushes. Once a world mutation starts, the consumed decision is never replayed, preserving the shared crash/rollback limitation.

SeaSalvage requires stable server-owned or unassigned item records and leaves active client-owned drops alone. It cannot cancel a pickup already completed on a client before the server's observations. The native ownership and record fence addresses stale updates during transfer; gameplay correctness across client timing and ownership transitions still depends on native replication. Native world saving owns cargo and consumed source data; normal save rollback affects both. Restore complete native save snapshots, rather than combining chunks from different saves. Recovery creates public, destructible floating cargo that can drift or lose contents through ordinary gameplay. A transferred-drop log confirms the server's recorded operation, not client visibility or successful player collection.

Return to [HeimKit](../README.md) or [server setup](server-setup.md).

## Building restoration and administration

HeimMender restores existing eligible health, including old combat damage: saved health cannot identify its original cause. Its automatic quiet-period and untamed-creature checks use server observations; they cannot guarantee combat has stopped. Native support collapse and future damage continue. Owner requests can be lost during ownership transitions; only subsequently observed full health is counted, and simultaneous manual repair cannot be attributed uniquely.

Unmodified clients cannot recognize a newly registered server console command. HeimMender terminal commands belong on the dedicated server; its live config request also works with vanilla clients. Ordinary chat can be handled entirely between clients or locally, particularly with one player, so a server-routed admin chat request is not guaranteed. Native admin-list connection authentication is required when it does arrive. Live configuration currently applies only to HeimMender; other settings and hidden shared debug suppression retain their restart requirements.

The new world features process bounded actor-relative scopes. Native sector collection can temporarily exceed the retained-record cap before rejection, and native scene/terrain preparation still incurs game-engine work. Caps limit retained state and per-pass operations; they do not establish a frame-time guarantee on dense worlds. ForestRenewal batches non-placement ledger updates, so a crash before their flush can forfeit a recently removed stump opportunity.

Debug controller-pass timing measures only synchronous controller work, with interval average/maximum milliseconds. It excludes native network callbacks, later terrain/scene creation, rendering and total server frame time, so it cannot alone establish overall server performance.
