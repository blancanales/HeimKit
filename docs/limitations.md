# Safety and limitations

This page collects HeimKit's gameplay, client and diagnostic limitations. Feature guides describe controls and supported behavior; specifications define implementation contracts.

## World safety

Use HeimKit at your own risk. Mods can corrupt worlds or cause data loss; we are not responsible for resulting damage. Back up the world before installing, updating or enabling HeimKit. Stop the server before replacing its DLL, editing configuration or copying save files. Follow [world backups](world-backups.md), including the separate SharedMap and ForestRenewal data directories.

Recorded upgrade checkpoints retain the world from their original capture; restarting does not refresh them with later gameplay. Missing or invalid records/archives can require a replacement from the current world and cannot reconstruct a lost earlier checkpoint. Only HeimKit's own matching record and verified archive establish this checkpoint; an unrelated backup filename does not establish its world/version identity. Automatic upgrade backups capture the local files present at the native pre-world-load boundary, before HeimKit gameplay modules activate. Game metadata selection, earlier game conversion work and other mods may already have run; this is not a backup from before installing the server upgrade. Unsupported or changed native save APIs prevent HeimKit activation rather than producing an assumed-complete copy. A newly created world without all required save files can first load under vanilla processing and receive its backup on a later startup. Backups on the same host do not protect against host/disk failure. Retention is manual, so provide free disk space and keep independent copies off-host. Restore with the matching game and loader environment where needed; no automatic rollback occurs.

## Server observations and logs

Background battle pauses start when native server random-event raid state exists, or authenticated nearby native damage traffic is observed against a Character target. An unattended or paused native raid retains its pause until the event is removed; this does not depend on AFKRaidWatch being enabled. It can miss windup, air swings, fights without server-visible damage messages and activity beyond its 128-metre observation range. Boss targeting extends pressure longer; it does not prove a boss is still alive or that damage succeeded. Eight-second ordinary and twenty-second boss windows renew with evidence. Optional new work stops entirely until both raid state and damage windows clear, then ordinary cadence resumes. Background discovery and map publication can be delayed during sustained fighting. Native calls, one metadata asset load, one table reconciliation and main-thread snapshot serialization are indivisible; the soft admission budget is not a frame-time guarantee. Active transactions, manual interactions, WeatherGuard protection and support/terrain upkeep and cleanup continue. Existing TameTravel animal movement/travel and explicitly requested administrative repairs retain their active service paths. Mandatory validation of accepted cartography uploads also continues; the automatic table pause does not reject player uploads.

Scene binding repair preserves world data and permits normal native re-creation; it does not reconstruct an already-lost client view or establish what originally destroyed the component. Normal performance summaries include scene pass/maintenance/retention measurements and combat pressure/deferral counts. These are elapsed scopes and can overlap. A zero native working-set counter is reported as unavailable rather than zero memory usage.

HeimKit works from server-observed state. Client ownership, unloaded objects and replication delays affect when changes are visible. Logs distinguish requests, accepted state and observed results; they do not confirm a player's screen or delivery to every client. Ready means native services are available. Compilation, compatibility checks and hook registration do not establish gameplay correctness.

Arrival diagnostics begin with server-observed character announcements before actor availability, then observe validated positions. They observe first character availability and position changes, not a confirmed login/portal operation or client loading-screen completion. The 0.2-second position sample and 64-session limit can miss short-distance teleports or intermediate travel; a jump of at least 64 metres within two seconds is inferred, while longer gaps are labeled separately. Character replacement and recovery are separate events. The initial two-second pin-diagnostic quiet period is a sampling rule, not proof of safety or completed loading. An unknown destination can temporarily withhold these optional comparisons without spatial certainty; player uploads and map normalization continue. Cold-start admission and incremental cell discovery cannot preempt a single native call or Harmony installation, so they do not guarantee a maximum frame duration. Ten-second windows end by observation timeout, never by a claim that loading finished. Server zone-loaded state is not proof of complete terrain, support or client readiness. First-observed times have sampling delay; missing cached records and stale protection/progress evidence remain unknown. Pending-work coverage includes only the named producers and does not enumerate every feature job. Shared elapsed work, callbacks between updates, independent stage maxima and concurrent arrivals cannot establish per-player CPU cost or the incremental cost compared with vanilla. The exact slowest-update phase breakdown is retained separately; arrival collection/output after that sample boundary is included in normal health timing instead. Output caps and early disconnect/world changes can leave an arrival partially observed.

Some diagnostic observers have incomplete incident repeat-count/recovery accounting. Debug output is sampled and capped. Missing detail lines do not establish missing processing. A replication sender may be forwarding another player's action, so sender identity does not prove who clicked a control. Other mods that patch the same systems can conflict. AllowUnverifiedVersions changes version policy, not the API audit. An accepted game version and successful backup cannot establish compatibility of every gameplay behavior, and a feature may change state before an error is detected. Feature isolation stops further processing; it does not undo prior changes. See [supported environment](server-setup.md#supported-environment).

Debug controller-pass timing measures only synchronous controller work, with interval average/maximum milliseconds. It excludes native network callbacks, later terrain/scene creation, rendering and total server frame time, so it cannot alone establish overall server performance.

The Verbose Debug performance window samples intervals between HeimKit Plugin.Update entries and measures its synchronous processing separately. Neither interval counts nor optional feature-pass counts are server FPS. Stopwatch scopes measure elapsed time, including scheduling, GC and synchronous listener pauses, rather than isolated CPU execution. Process CPU-time deltas include the entire process and cannot attribute a stall to HeimKit. Native-context scopes cover explicit zone/context calls; components, physics and later callbacks can add work outside them. Stage and scope maxima can belong to different passes and must not be added to reconstruct a frame. Shared frame-discovery counts cover cached cells, not a whole-world census. Unsupported process counters are omitted.

The work preceding the largest observed update gap is associated evidence, not proof that it caused the gap; native work and scheduling outside the measured update can intervene. Concurrent log-counter observations near a reporting boundary are sampled across adjacent windows. Basic summaries do not require or establish a reproducible multiplayer diagnosis.

Cooperative scheduling bounds admission of new work, not the duration of an already running native call or one table payload. Deferred passes and optional output can appear later or be omitted. A slow listener can exceed the optional-output allowance in one call; the gate prevents further optional writes that update without interrupting the listener or hiding required failures. Logger timing covers HeimKit's own DiagnosticLog calls, not all native Unity or other-mod output. Use [performance observations](server-setup.md#performance-observations) to distinguish these boundaries.

Persistent feature protection records disabling exceptions, not unobserved gameplay errors or a diagnosis that a server update caused the failure. It cannot detect silent incorrect behavior or undo earlier world changes. Healthy startup does not exercise every gameplay path. Decisions apply only to the recorded world/save location and HeimKit/Valheim versions; a changed combination is evaluated separately. An operator reset retries the feature without establishing that its cause is fixed. See [persistent feature disables](server-setup.md#persistent-feature-disables).

## SharedMap

Periodic table results arrive after bounded processing completes. A newer upload or pin-state change cancels an older job; later reconciliation retries it. Native asset admission, a single pin callback, native world query, allocation or compression unit may still exceed a soft slice budget. Performance logs measure elapsed work; fresh gameplay observations are needed to assess the effect on lag.

### Discovery

Exploration uses periodic, replicated player positions. Sampling can miss travel between observations; teleports reveal endpoint circles only. Dead, unavailable and interior-height positions do not contribute. Camera visibility is not evaluated.

Resource, dungeon, runestone, spawner and Dvergr structure discovery waits for native records. An installed prefab does not mean the location or deposit exists in a particular world. Unplaced dungeon centers do not establish exterior entrances. Retrospective discovery uses accumulated community exploration, including imported table terrain; enabling it can reveal previously visited locations without a revisit.

Silver mining discovery depends on authenticated impacts or correlated digging observations. Missing baselines, delayed data or simultaneous nearby tools can leave a vein hidden until another qualifying impact. Missing/unloaded resource records do not prove depletion; unobserved destruction can leave historical markers. Copper/Silver/Black Marble percentages measure remaining mining sections, not material yield, exposed volume or burial. Black Marble markers cover outdoor petrified skull/rib resources, not ruins, dungeon marble, giant weapons/armour or player structures. Discovery files saved with the new BlackMarble resource kind require a build that supports it; earlier builds cannot read that kind. See [SharedMap backups](world-backups.md#sharedmap-data) before downgrading.

Ancient Root percentages describe the last accepted replicated root reserve, rounded down to a whole percent, not extractor fill, installation, extraction readiness or a live forecast. The server does not simulate offline regeneration or force a root to load; values can remain stale until a native update is observed. Discovery uses ordinary sampled positions or the configured community-exploration retrospective rule, not camera visibility. A temporary 0% reserve is not terminal depletion. Individual root markers still follow resource-marker proximity hiding and vanilla Read Map behavior. Stores containing root discoveries use an extended resource format that older builds cannot read; retain [SharedMap backups](world-backups.md#sharedmap-data) before downgrading.

A dungeon percentage measures only its listed permanent pickup sites, not creature kills, chest contents or overall dungeon completion. Troll Cave markers do not report whether their Troll was defeated. Picking a site releases its item; it does not prove the item reached an inventory. Interior visits use sampled, living player positions and a unique saved location in the native interior zone; ambiguous matches do not unlock percentages. Matched already-picked items can also establish a prior visit; they do not identify who entered or when. Empty pickup records require a settling period after entry because replication can arrive late. Missing layouts, missing previously established records and unsupported objectives remain unknown. Respawning mushrooms/Jelly, random chest contents and destroyed pickups are excluded. Percentages already copied to personal maps by an earlier build cannot be withdrawn by the server.

Runestones use validated placed-proxy hierarchy offsets. Native StaticPhysics may settle their height locally; their horizontal landmark point remains fixed. Their static lore objects have no individual destruction record. Spawner markers require visible, player-destructible physical objects with actual persistent source records; an installed or historically registered prefab does not prove world presence. Only accepted source-ZDO destruction is retirement evidence. Destruction missed while processing is paused, before compatible metadata is available, or while the plugin is absent can leave a historical marker. Killing spawned creatures and scene unloading never retire a source. Spawner vertical settling retains the accepted source height; simultaneously stacked sources sharing one horizontal identity are omitted as ambiguous. Exact source reassociation rejects ambiguity; moved/replaced sources without a unique accepted identity retain the last state.

Interior runestones/spawners are omitted: publication requires verified saved-layout membership and an established exterior gateway association, which this marker path does not provide. High-altitude coordinates are never projected onto the outdoor map. Outdoor placement of an otherwise eligible physical source can qualify; this does not imply complete interior support. Unsupported animated/conditional hierarchies and excessive replicated scales are omitted.

Dvergr structure discovery requires native exploration range or saved community exploration at the placed point. Structures outside both remain hidden. Recorded markers persist if the structure is destroyed.

### Client map updates

The client’s house-icon filter hides Dvergr structure markers together with player house pins. A personal copy of the retired Infested Citadel hammer pin stays on that player’s map until they delete it. Server cleanup applies to shared table data.

Players receive updates through Read Map after table reconciliation and replication. An early read may use older data. The server cannot force an open map to refresh or edit a personal client map.

Vanilla matches an incoming marker to any saved pin within one horizontal metre, without updating its label or icon. Personal pins can block insertion; distinct entrances closer than that can appear as one client pin. SharedMap additionally suppresses dungeon publications within ten horizontal metres of a stable actual-point anchor. Recognized dungeon-labeled hammer copies in that area are cleaned from shared table data; genuine exceptionally close entrances can therefore share one publication while retaining separate histories. Local stale-pin deletion followed by Read Map may be necessary. Portal/resource rename offsets and dungeon percentage/shared-checkmark offsets encourage replacement, but missed updates, reused slots, personal pins and regrouping can retain stale labels or checkmarks. Checkmark changes require allowed Write Map uploads; a stale or unchanged upload is not an explicit reversal. Personal pins belonging to the reader remain under that player's control. Publication offsets add at most about 2.83 metres; portal/resource rename offsets can add their own displacement.

### Player uploads

Write Map reads the table before uploading. Deleting a pin locally may therefore re-import it; later uploads from another player can restore it. Use [selected-pin removal](features/shared-map.md#removing-an-unwanted-shared-pin) for persistent table exclusions.

Checked pins are client-local until Write Map. With a client-owned table, the server may see its owner forwarding another player's upload. Native owner/author/display-name fields, proximity and table ownership cannot authenticate who clicked X. Automatic admin checked-pin deletion is unavailable; use server-side selected-pin administration. Shared X marks remain governed by SharePinCheckmarks. Table removals reach other players through shared data; already copied personal pins and the uploader's adopted X remain client-owned and may need local removal. Unrecognized ownership edits are preserved as ordinary pins rather than authorizing a global deletion.

AllowPlayerMapWrites=false rejects incoming server map fields. Vanilla still displays Write Map, its effects and its success message. A client owner can complete a local write/read before server correction; rejection cannot undo that read.

### Saved data

Community terrain, discoveries and marker state are separate from the native world save. A crash can lose changes since the last completed save. Restoring an older world with newer community data can reveal later progress. Corrupt or incompatible stores disable SharedMap instead of silently resetting data. Back up and restore matching files together.

## AFK detection, sleep and raids

Idle is an inference, not proof of human absence. Camera-only movement and unsent menu, map or inventory actions can be invisible. Unknown or unreliable observations retain native eligibility and restart inference grace. Sleep still depends on native bed/time checks; a permitted vote does not prove that a skip completed. Raid exclusion counts describe candidate filtering, not a canceled raid or a raid that was certain to occur. Ordinary enemies and existing raids remain active.

## WeatherGuard and InfiniteTorches

WeatherGuard coverage depends on loaded server context and ownership. Roof samples and coverage reports describe their stated regions and ages, not the whole world. Server weather is not a measurement of each client's local rain or Wet status; prevention waits for native damage timing. Context readiness currently examines horizontal sectors without separating vertically distant geometry; an unloaded interior or other elevated object sharing those sectors can delay outdoor protection even when it cannot affect the building. Missing terrain or relevant server instances also leaves eligible pieces waiting until that context is ready. During candidate fallback, a cached readiness result can lag a newly arrived dependency by up to one second. With scene-list reuse enabled, a distant-only record that gains its distant flag can wait until the next list rebuild, by the next protection cycle or at most 30 frames.

Torch refilling can wait for loaded records, ownership and replication. Late delivery can overlap timeout retries. Native water, smoke and obstruction checks still affect whether a fueled light burns. Simultaneous manual refilling can consume its normal material, and an observed fuel increase cannot uniquely identify which operation caused it.

## TameTravel

An explicit follow command can reclaim a stale saved follower only when that player is absent from a reliable connection snapshot. Reconnection or a changed saved follower cancels the pending takeover; no saved name alone grants portal permission. Static ruin floors retain the same thickness, height, footprint, overhead-clearance and access-route requirements as player paving.

Stopping may briefly refresh the animal's visible client instance to remove cached follow targets; its authoritative identity and saved state remain unchanged. Confirmation requires native client ordering acknowledgements, which do not prove rendering on a particular screen. A disconnected or delayed connection can defer confirmation. Recovery only clears saved state present in the last world save. Older unmarked wolves cannot be distinguished from vanilla followers and retain their following until a fresh authenticated stay request. Other mods that assign following to normally non-commandable animals overlap the legacy startup cleanup.

Validated portal traversal combines fresh authenticated following, source trigger geometry, unchanged portal links and matching arrival. Unmodified clients publish positions rather than a portal-use event. An owner-local administrative teleport that reproduces the same observations can be indistinguishable. The public map animates markers toward position updates; its fast motion is not a physical travel path. Map visibility is not required for TameTravel.

Accepted owner updates are retained between controller passes, but movement absent from replication cannot be reconstructed. A fresh inside-trigger point can establish source presence without an observed outside boundary. Stale or overflowing history and a jump without source evidence withhold travel. Dense rosters can exceed the bounded processing window. A six-second retained source witness accommodates some asynchronous native portal-link updates; unresolved or changing links and ambiguous usable triggers still withhold transfer. A cancelled attempt consumes optional single-trip waiting consent. Active follow permission survives an attempt while the same valid player session and native following remain; neither follow permission nor interrupted transfers are reconstructed after reconnect or restart.

Arrival timing, continued presence, reference position and native ping echoes constrain readiness but do not expose completion of the client's loading screen. Narrow trigger crossings, packet delays, dense player rosters, missing terrain/scene objects, duplicate names or insufficient animal-sized clearance can withhold transfer. A follow request is not a confirmed result. Chat communication restrictions, recipient routing and owner-local wolf commands can hide input from the server. Use the vanilla Come Here/No No No/Wave emotes to provide replicated nearby follow/stay/wait input. Unknown custom slash commands never reach the server. An explicit follow association is required; standing livestock never gains permission from proximity alone. Remaining followers can stay at the source when a journey times out or the player leaves the destination area. A pending stop revokes travel permission before native stay confirmation, which can still fail if ownership, the player instance or native command handling is unavailable. An unresolved earlier follow command also withholds stay confirmation rather than treating its initial empty state as success.

Ownership barriers preserve the same animal record and fence stale updates during transfer; the forced native source-record return reconciles cached persistent data, while private state absent from that record remains unavailable. Interactions with reserved animals pause briefly during the handoff. Recorded relocation, ordered message reception and observed destination-owner simulation are different outcomes. Interrupted handoffs can leave the original animal at its destination without confirmed resumed simulation; they never authorize a replacement copy. Native world saving owns final location and animal data, so crashes/restored saves can roll back recent travel. No consent or interrupted journey is resumed after restart.

Native following uses a fixed three-dimensional centre distance. TameTravel can replace that movement intent only while it owns an explicitly commanded follower and has ready terrain/collision context. Capacity refusal, a failed source-state drain, riding, player separation beyond thirty-two metres or lost context releases control to native behavior. After separation, control returns only once the animal is back within sixteen metres. A received follow confirmation alone does not establish active spacing; a separate HUD/log result identifies activation. Adjusted spacing does not prevent combat approaches, knockback, all collision pushes, or resolve animal pathfinding through narrow doors and stairs. Brief ownership/context transitions pause simulation and interactions; repeated commands during those transitions may be withheld. Portal waiting avoids needing an animal to pathfind into a building. Native stay/patrol can wander. Native physics and pathfinding now run on the server for retained followers; controller and follow-movement timings do not measure all of that cost. The follow-movement timer includes synchronous native MoveTo work, but excludes other AI, physics and work scheduled later. Native private client AI caches are not copied by record draining.

Rejected command selection appears in TameTravel Debug evidence; emote start/stop state can still be coalesced by client replication. `/nonono` addresses only the sender's saved followers or current associations within thirty-two metres and does not grant portal permission after reconnect.

TameTravel samples arcs around the traveller out to twelve metres before wider bounded grids. Padding and its push use only nearby colliders and horizontal penetration; opposing walls in a narrow gap cancel the push, and spots crowded by more than thirty-two colliders are rejected. Low structural paving may support a landing; furniture, tall construction, overhead pieces and unsupported footprints do not. Narrow gaps between samples, the deadline, missing collision context or unavailable native navigation can still withhold placement despite visible space. The access check uses a player-sized native route with current physical wall checks and a bounded detour allowance. It permits building doorways, but does not prove the animal itself can fit through those doorways or stairs. Landing checks also cover its actual body, footprint and a bounded walkout; they cannot prevent later obstruction by players, creatures or construction. A server-record or ownership acknowledgement does not prove that a particular client has rendered the animal.

## New world features

DoorKeeper, ForestRenewal and SeaSalvage work from server-observed state and require ready native world context. Loading, dense sector scopes, missing prefab definitions, ownership changes and replication delays can postpone work. Rotating player scopes bound processing; many players or large builds can increase delays. Position and movement-segment exclusion checks cannot predict an unreported player movement or guarantee what a client currently sees.

DoorKeeper retains native surrounding objects before taking door ownership so native support, weather and damage handlers have their context. Preparation can take multiple native scene-creation frames. Manual interactions during ownership transitions still depend on native delivery. Close timing begins from observed open state and resets after controller recreation; it is not an exact measurement of when a player clicked. Door clearance automatically expands a conservative sphere around the native collision bounds for wide/tall gates; this can delay closure until players move farther away.

ForestRenewal discovers standing stumps and observes native stump destruction while occupied. Old stumps start their timer when first observed. A pending standing stump that disappears without an observed destruction event waits; missed removals are not reconstructed from absence. Structure buffers use conservative saved collision bounds and are rechecked at sapling placement, including new buildings during the delay. Unsupported/missing geometry defers work; this is not continuous enforcement around later construction or existing mature trees. Paint exclusions cover detectable dirt/paved/cultivated ground, not every player-used path. The separate world-specific renewal ledger must be backed up with the world. Consuming a renewal before placing its sapling prevents retries from duplicating it; a crash before the subsequent native world save can forfeit that renewal. Corrupt ledgers stop renewal. Restoring mismatched world/ledger snapshots can suppress later renewals. Persisted decisions are bounded; reaching the ledger capacity skips new stump observations/removals. Failed native plant growth is not refunded or bypassed.

ForestRenewal rechecks placement exclusions after durable acknowledgement. Confirmed new exclusions cancel renewal; missing context before any world mutation returns the original decision to pending after sixty seconds without rerolling. Orderly shutdown returns unperformed placements to pending and waits for outstanding ledger I/O; normal gameplay does not wait on disk flushes. Once a world mutation starts, the consumed decision is never replayed, preserving the shared crash/rollback limitation.

SeaSalvage requires stable server-owned or unassigned item records and leaves active client-owned drops alone. It cannot cancel a pickup already completed on a client before the server's observations. The native ownership and record fence addresses stale updates during transfer; gameplay correctness across client timing and ownership transitions still depends on native replication. Native world saving owns cargo and consumed source data; normal save rollback affects both. Restore complete native save snapshots, rather than combining chunks from different saves. Recovery creates public, destructible floating cargo that can drift or lose contents through ordinary gameplay. A transferred-drop log confirms the server's recorded operation, not client visibility or successful player collection.

The new world features process bounded actor-relative scopes. Native sector collection can temporarily exceed the retained-record cap before rejection, and native scene/terrain preparation still incurs game-engine work. Caps limit retained state and per-pass operations; they do not establish a frame-time guarantee on dense worlds. ForestRenewal batches non-placement ledger updates, so a crash before their flush can forfeit a recently removed stump opportunity.

## Building restoration and administration

HeimMender restores existing eligible health, including old combat damage: saved health cannot identify its original cause. Its automatic quiet-period and untamed-creature checks use server observations; they cannot guarantee combat has stopped. Gradual repair timing is tracked only while a ready player stays within scan range: a damaged piece left unobserved for three minutes starts a new repair wait when it is next observed, and continuing damage such as unprotected rain wear restarts the wait each time. Native support collapse and future damage continue. Owner requests can be lost during ownership transitions; only subsequently observed full health is counted, and simultaneous manual repair cannot be attributed uniquely.

Unmodified clients cannot recognize a newly registered server console command. HeimMender terminal commands belong on the dedicated server; its live config request also works with vanilla clients. Ordinary chat can be handled entirely between clients or locally, particularly with one player, so a server-routed admin chat request is not guaranteed. Native admin-list connection authentication is required when it does arrive. Live configuration currently applies only to HeimMender; other settings and hidden shared debug suppression retain their restart requirements.

Return to [HeimKit](../README.md) or [server setup](server-setup.md).

## StationSupply

Nearest-chest assignment describes placement, not item availability. An empty or busy nearest chest deliberately blocks fallback. Horizontal intake projection accepts floor-level chests below tall hoppers; the separate height tolerance does not establish line of sight or accessibility through walls. Native prefab geometry is used; scaled or changed modded definitions are outside the supported contract. Ward checks cover native wards and conservatively require permission for every overlapping enabled ward; custom protection mods may enforce different rules.

Transfers require current owner state and native replication echoes. Unknown ownership, unavailable player scope, open inventories or missing native state delay/cancel feeding. Successful source/destination mutations describe server state; native world saving still determines crash persistence. Client cache refresh reuses the original record identity and can briefly refresh a station or closed chest visually. Fermenter station instances receive ordinary state updates to preserve native delayed tapping output. Echoes establish message ordering, not rendering on a particular screen. A refresh timeout does not reverse a completed transfer. Competing inventory/production mods that change the same records or native methods can conflict.

Manual container requests take priority and pause further automation for ten seconds after observed use. A request caught during an inventory fence still waits for safe source draining or a committed transfer's client refresh, followed by native ownership and access checks. The first request is retained for at most fifteen seconds; repeated clicks do not add more queued requests. A disconnect, replaced object, unavailable owner, unsafe cancellation or expiry can require another click. Native access restrictions and another player's open inventory can still refuse access. Ordered delivery does not establish that a remote client has created or displayed the container view.

Obliterator feeding uses a dedicated disposal chest selected by proximity. All categories are initially selected, so enable it only for a chest intended to supply disposal items. Categories follow native types/skills and observed cooking/fermenting conversion metadata; they describe groups rather than player intent. Unrecognized registered item types belong to Other. Native lever request/animation evidence and local busy state defer feeding, but a client stalled beyond the observed quiet window can have its manual lever cycle canceled by ownership handoff and need another pull. The module never pulls the lever or automatically collects coal.

Production stays native. Feeding is bounded, pauses during sleep skipping and stops when no ready player is nearby; it does not promise continuous operation at every production rate. Fermenter cover checks wait for surrounding native context and never collect finished mead. Physical chest layout, clear output space and manual access remain the players' responsibility.

StationSupply's candidate and transaction limits bound work counts, rather than guaranteeing a fixed number of milliseconds or zero server overhead. Base density, inventory payloads and native replication affect cost. Its cached negative-input decisions rely on native data-revision updates; changes become eligible again when observed, without bypassing ownership validation for a transfer. Ineligible stations also consume the station-candidate budget, so crowded scopes can take longer to rotate through. Optional timing measures controller work only; full server frame, networking and Unity physics costs require live observation.

Stage timings describe synchronous calls in the reporting window. Stage maxima can come from different passes, and stages can include nested cleanup or diagnostic work; adding their maxima does not reconstruct one server frame. Cache-reset and source-state request counts are operation calls, not measured packet counts or network bytes. Optional samples remain bounded; actionable incident warnings and activity counters provide separate evidence when detailed samples are omitted.

Native asynchronous asset loading removes HeimKit's synchronous asset wait, but the engine can still spend main-thread time integrating a loaded asset or inspecting its hierarchy. Asset wait durations include pauses in cooperative admission and are not isolated CPU time. Scene-scoped pure metadata is reused until scene replacement or plugin teardown. Native save-request and preparation observations do not prove background file writing completed; a preserved native save exception still needs investigation.

Player-context diagnostics are delayed, bounded samples, not complete combat tracking. They record damage-time positions separately from later player positions, and keep damage-source and targeted-notification-source identities separate. Routed traffic does not prove a successful hit, the attacker's identity or hostility; locally handled client actions may never reach the server. Native OnTargeted notifications do not identify the NPC. Exact NPC targets are inspected only for a small sample of already-cached server-owned live AI; building/static targets and client-owned unknowns are reported separately. Server-native weather is not proof of weather at a remote player. Nearby WeatherGuard counts have explicit freshness, coverage and native-event windows; absent events never establish dry/safe conditions everywhere. Shared frame workload cannot be assigned uniquely to one player or region.
